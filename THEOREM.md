# Common-multiplier 91 obstruction at length 115

## Theorem

There is no binary Legendre pair \((a,b)\in\{\pm1\}^{115}\times\{\pm1\}^{115}\) for which \(91\) belongs to the multiplier group of both sequences.

Equivalently, after independent cyclic shifts, there is no length-115 Legendre pair whose two sequences are both fixed by decimation by \(91\).

## Proof

Normalize a hypothetical Legendre pair so that both sign sequences have sum \(1\). Let \(X,Y\subseteq\mathbb Z_{115}\) be the positions of the \(-1\) entries. Then

\[
|X|=|Y|=57,
\]

and the Legendre equations are equivalent to the supplementary-difference-set relations

\[
N_X(t)+N_Y(t)=56\qquad(t\ne0),
\]

where \(N_X(t)=|X\cap(X-t)|\).

The standard multiplier-normalization lemma applies because the number of \(+1\) entries is \(58\) and \(\gcd(58,115)=1\). Thus, if \(91\) is a multiplier of both sequences, independent cyclic shifts preserve the Legendre equations and may be chosen so that

\[
91X=X,\qquad91Y=Y.
\]

### Compression modulo 23

For \(j\in\mathbb Z_{23}\), put

\[
x_j=|\{r\in\mathbb Z_5:j+23r\in X\}|,
\qquad
y_j=|\{r\in\mathbb Z_5:j+23r\in Y\}|.
\]

Since \(91\equiv-1\pmod{23}\), both compressed vectors are symmetric:

\[
x_j=x_{-j},\qquad y_j=y_{-j}.
\]

Write \(C_x(s)=\sum_jx_jx_{j+s}\). For \(s\ne0\) modulo 23, the five lifts of \(s\) to \(\mathbb Z_{115}\) are all nonzero, hence

\[
C_x(s)+C_y(s)=5\cdot56=280.
\]

For \(s=0\), one lift is zero and four are nonzero, so

\[
C_x(0)+C_y(0)=2\cdot57+4\cdot56=338.
\]

### Parity collapse

Reduce \(x,y\) coefficientwise modulo two and regard the results as elements \(p,q\) of \(\mathbb F_2[\mathbb Z_{23}]\). Every combined correlation coefficient above is even, so

\[
p(z)p(z^{-1})+q(z)q(z^{-1})=0.
\]

Symmetry gives \(p(z^{-1})=p(z)\) and \(q(z^{-1})=q(z)\), hence

\[
(p+q)^2=0.
\]

In characteristic two, squaring sends \(\sum c_jz^j\) to \(\sum c_jz^{2j}\). Because multiplication by \(2\) permutes \(\mathbb Z_{23}\), this map is injective. Therefore \(p=q\), i.e.

\[
x_j\equiv y_j\pmod2\qquad\text{for all }j.
\]

Set

\[
u=(x+y)/2,\qquad v=(x-y)/2.
\]

Then \(u,v\in\mathbb Z^{23}\), both are symmetric,

\[
\sum_j u_j=57,\qquad\sum_jv_j=0,
\]

and

\[
C_u(s)+C_v(s)=
\begin{cases}
169,&s=0,\\
140,&s\ne0.
\end{cases}
\]

### A norm equation in the real 23rd cyclotomic field

Let \(\zeta\) be a primitive 23rd root of unity and put

\[
\alpha=\sum_{j=0}^{22}u_j\zeta^j,
\qquad
\beta=\sum_{j=0}^{22}v_j\zeta^j.
\]

Because \(u\) and \(v\) are symmetric, \(\alpha,\beta\) lie in the totally real field

\[
K=\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}),\qquad[K:\mathbb Q]=11,
\]

and are algebraic integers. Fourier transformation of the combined correlation identity at a nontrivial character yields

\[
\alpha^2+\beta^2
=169+140\sum_{s=1}^{22}\zeta^s
=29.
\]

Let \(L=K(i)\) and \(\gamma=\alpha+i\beta\). Then

\[
\gamma\overline\gamma=29,
\]

where the bar is complex conjugation over \(K\).

The rational prime \(29\) is inert in \(K\): \(29\equiv6\pmod{23}\) has multiplicative order \(11\), and because that order is odd no smaller power is \(-1\) modulo 23. On the other hand \(-1\) is a square modulo 29 (for example \(12^2\equiv-1\pmod{29}\)), so the unique prime of \(K\) above 29 splits in \(L/K\). Its two primes are principal, generated by \(5+2i\) and \(5-2i\), since

\[
(5+2i)(5-2i)=29
\]

and their absolute ideal norms are \(29^{11}\).

It follows from \((\gamma)(\overline\gamma)=(29)\) that

\[
\gamma=\varepsilon(5+2i)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\gamma=\varepsilon(5-2i)
\]

for a unit \(\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_L^\times\) with \(\varepsilon\overline\varepsilon=1\).

For every embedding \(\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\),

\[
|\sigma(\varepsilon)|^2
=\sigma(\varepsilon\overline\varepsilon)=1.
\]

Kronecker's theorem therefore makes \(\varepsilon\) a root of unity. Moreover

\[
L=K(i)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{23},i)=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{92}).
\]

Because 92 is divisible by 4, the roots of unity in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{92})\) have order dividing 92. A primitive 92nd root cannot lie in \(L\), since its cyclotomic field has degree 44 while \([L:\mathbb Q]=22\). If a primitive 23rd (or 46th) root lay in \(L\), then the degree-22 field \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{23})\) would equal \(L\), forcing \(i\in\mathbb Q(\zeta_{23})\); this is impossible because \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{23})\) is unramified at 2 whereas \(\mathbb Q(i)\) is ramified at 2. Thus the roots of unity in \(L\) are exactly

\[
\{\pm1,\pm i\}.
\]

Hence the real part \(\alpha\) belongs to

\[
\{\pm2,\pm5\}.
\]

### Final cyclotomic congruence

Let

\[
U(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{22}u_jz^j\in\mathbb Z[z].
\]

Since \(U(\zeta)=\alpha=c\) for some \(c\in\{\pm2,\pm5\}\), irreducibility of \(\Phi_{23}\) and \(\deg(U-c)\le22\) imply

\[
U(z)-c=m\Phi_{23}(z)
\]

for an integer \(m\). Evaluating at \(z=1\) gives

\[
57-c=23m.
\]

Therefore \(c\equiv57\equiv11\pmod{23}\), whereas the four possible residues of \(c\) are \(2,21,5,18\). Contradiction.

So no length-115 binary Legendre pair can have 91 as a multiplier of both sequences. \(\square\)

## Scope

The theorem excludes one common-multiplier symmetry class only. It does **not** prove that binary Legendre pairs of length 115 do not exist, and it does not exclude pairs with trivial multiplier groups, different nontrivial multiplier groups, or the other common involution classes except by separate arguments not included in this frozen claim.
